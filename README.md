Field: Clinical Pharmacy and Pharmacovigilance
Course: Doctor of Pharmacy (Pharm.D)

1. Introduction

An Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is a harmful and unintended response to a medicine when it is used at normal doses.

ADR reporting is an important part of pharmacovigilance because it helps identify potential medicine-related risks and improves patient safety.

This project demonstrates how to document a suspected adverse drug reaction using a structured reporting form.

2. Objectives

1. To understand the importance of ADR reporting.
2. To collect relevant patient and medication information.
3. To document suspected adverse reactions accurately.
4. To record the onset and outcome of the reaction.
5. To understand the role of pharmacists in pharmacovigilance.

3. Case Scenario

Note: The following is a fictional educational example, not a real patient report.

A 35-year-old female patient was prescribed amoxicillin for a bacterial infection. After taking the medicine, she developed a skin rash and itching.

She contacted her healthcare provider, who assessed her symptoms and advised appropriate management.

The suspected reaction was documented for pharmacovigilance assessment.

5. Documentation and Assessment

The patient's reported skin rash and itching following exposure to amoxicillin raise suspicion of a possible adverse drug reaction.

However, the available information is insufficient to establish that amoxicillin caused the reaction.

Further assessment should consider:

- The time between drug administration and symptom onset.
- Previous allergy history.
- Other medicines and possible alternative causes.
- Clinical findings and treatment provided.
- Patient outcome after appropriate management.

A causality category should not be assigned without adequate case information.

6. ADR Reporting Process

Identification of suspected ADR
↓
Collection of patient information
↓
Documentation of the suspected medicine
↓
Recording of the adverse reaction and its timeline
↓
Collection of relevant medical history
↓
Assessment of seriousness and causality
↓
Completion and review of the ADR reporting form
↓
Submission to the appropriate pharmacovigilance reporting centre
↓
Follow-up and monitoring, when required

7. Role of the Pharmacist

1. Identify and document suspected ADRs.
2. Collect accurate medication histories.
3. Educate patients about potential adverse effects.
4. Report suspected ADRs through appropriate channels.
5. Support causality assessment by collecting relevant clinical information.
6. Promote the safe and rational use of medicines.

8. Importance of ADR Reporting

- Helps identify previously unrecognized medicine-related risks.
- Supports continuous monitoring of medicine safety.
- Helps healthcare professionals make informed treatment decisions.
- Contributes to pharmacovigilance databases.
- Promotes patient safety and rational drug use.

9. Conclusion

ADR reporting and documentation are essential components of pharmacovigilance. Accurate collection of patient details, suspected medication information, reaction characteristics, and clinical outcomes supports the evaluation of medicine safety.

Pharmacists play an important role in identifying, documenting, and reporting suspected adverse drug reactions.

Project outcome: A structured ADR documentation workflow was developed using a fictional case to demonstrate the essential components of pharmacovigilance reporting.
